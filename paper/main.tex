\begin{abstract}
We conduct an extensive, pre-registered out-of-sample investigation into whether statistical arbitrage strategies survive in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) single-stock Futures \& Options (F\&O) universe over the 16-year period from 2010 through 2025. Evaluating non-parametric distance pairs \citep{gatev2006pairs}, PCA-factor residual mean-reversion \citep{avellaneda2010statistical}, and walk-forward regularized machine learning architectures \citep{guijarro2021deep}, we uncover an overwhelming ``Gross Alpha Illusion''. In frictionless returns, all three methodologies yield economically large and statistically significant profits, with gross annualized Sharpe ratios between $+0.95$ and $+1.74$ (Newey--West $t$-statistics from $+4.05$ to $+7.49$). However, after incorporating realistic dated statutory Indian transaction costs---including the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) increased in October 2024, stamp duty, turnover charges, GST, and liquidity-tiered slippage---net annualized Sharpe ratios collapse universally into negative territory ($-1.63$ to $-2.96$), resulting in annual capital depletion of $-16.7\%$ to $-25.9\%$. The average breakeven cost multiplier is $0.38\times$ of baseline institutional costs. Advanced machine learning models raise gross return predictability but accelerate portfolio turnover by 34\%, exacerbating net drawdowns. All 12 pre-registered robustness variations fail the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ($\text{DSR} = 0.000$) and False Discovery Rate adjustments. We conclude that daily statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock F\&O is unviable as an independent standalone profit center, but retains substantial commercial value as an execution timing alpha overlay for institutional asset managers.
\end{abstract}
