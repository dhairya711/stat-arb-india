\section{Data and Universe Construction}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Point-in-Time F\&O Universe and Survivorship Bias}
To eliminate survivorship bias, our investment universe is reconstituted dynamically each calendar month to reflect exclusively those equity securities permitted for trading in the NSE F\&O segment. Over the 2010--2025 sample period, the size of this tradeable universe varies between 140 and 215 stocks (mean $= 182$ stocks). 

Crucially, companies that were subsequently banned, excluded, or delisted from the F\&O list (such as RCOM, Reliance Power, or DHFL) are retained in the historical cross-section up to their exact official exclusion date. When an open pair position contains a constituent stock that is announced for expulsion, the position is liquidated at the final settlement price of the active contract, mirroring institutional execution constraints.

\subsection{Corporate Action Adjustments}
Historical closing prices are adjusted backwards for all corporate actions, including stock splits, bonus issues, and cash dividends:
\begin{equation}
    P_{i,t}^{\text{adj}} = P_{i,t} \times \prod_{\tau > t} \left(1 - \frac{D_{i,\tau}}{P_{i,\tau - 1}}\right) \times \frac{1}{\text{SplitRatio}_\tau}
\end{equation}
The adjustment algorithms were validated against benchmark corporate actions across large-cap Indian stocks, including Infosys (INFY 1:1 bonus issues in 2014, 2015, and 2018) and Reliance Industries (splits and bonus events).

\subsection{Factor Benchmarks and Indian Risk Premia}
To evaluate whether statistical arbitrage returns represent true idiosyncratic alpha or compensation for systematic risk, we utilize daily factor returns from the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad (IIMA) four-factor model \citep{agarwalla2014four}, an emerging-market adaptation of the standard \citet{fama1993common} and \citet{carhart1997persistence} factor pricing specifications, comprising:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Market risk premium ($\text{MKT} = R_m - R_f$),
    \item Size factor ($\text{SMB}$),
    \item Value factor ($\text{HML}$),
    \item Momentum factor ($\text{WML}$), and
    \item 91-day Government of India Treasury Bill rate as the risk-free rate ($R_f$).
\end{itemize}
