\section{Conclusion and Practical Takeaways}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper provides a 16-year empirical look at statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock derivatives across distance pairs, PCA factor residuals, and machine learning models.

Four practical takeaways stand out:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Statistical Signals vs.\ Trading Reality:} While mean-reverting signals exist in Indian stocks with strong statistical confidence ($t > 4.0$), the typical spread deviation is smaller than the cost of entering and exiting the trade.
    \item \textbf{The Tax Barrier:} Mandatory Indian taxes (STT, stamp duty, GST, turnover charges) along with modest bid-ask slippage impose 12.0 to 18.5 basis points of friction per completed pairs cycle. For active daily strategies, this creates an annual drag exceeding 18\%, turning theoretical gains into severe net losses.
    \item \textbf{The Machine Learning Trade-Off:} Machine learning models do a better job of identifying short-term price moves on paper, but they increase portfolio turnover by 34\%. This extra churn creates even heavier net losses, showing that algorithmic complexity cannot overcome fixed statutory friction \citep{novymarx2016taxonomy}.
    \item \textbf{Value as an Execution Timing Tool:} Even though statistical arbitrage cannot work as an independent profit center in Indian single-stock F\&O, the residual signals are genuinely useful for execution desks. Portfolio managers who already need to rebalance long positions can use Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residuals to time their orders, potentially saving 20 to 40 basis points in execution costs.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, SEBI's October 2024 increase in Securities Transaction Tax raises the cost bar even higher for quantitative market-neutral strategies. As statutory friction grows, high-turnover arbitrage capital inevitably pulls back, with potential implications for relative price efficiency across Indian derivative markets.

\subsection{Managerial and Policy Implications for Decision Makers}
As the primary forum for decision makers in Indian management and policy, Vikalpa provides an ideal context for translating our empirical findings into actionable recommendations for market regulators and institutional asset managers.


\subsubsection*{Implications for Market Regulators (SEBI and the Ministry of Finance)}
The primary regulatory motivation behind the successive increases in Securities Transaction Tax—culminating in the October 2024 rate hike—has been the curb of excessive retail speculative participation in equity derivatives. SEBI's landmark study in 2023 revealed that nine out of ten individual retail traders incur consistent net losses in F\&O trading, prompting policymakers to use transaction taxes as a macroprudential dampener.

However, our empirical findings demonstrate that punitive transaction taxes impose serious unintended collateral damage on institutional relative-value arbitrageurs. Market-neutral quantitative strategies provide critical economic public goods to financial markets: they narrow bid-ask spreads, supply cross-sectional liquidity, and ensure that relative equity prices do not deviate persistently from economic fundamentals. By imposing a uniform turnover tax that fails to distinguish between unhedged naked speculative bets and market-neutral paired arbitrage, regulatory tax policy effectively expels disciplined quantitative arbitrageurs from the market. In the absence of viable arbitrage capital, cross-sectional mispricings persist longer, and price discovery efficiency degrades.

To reconcile investor protection with market efficiency, policymakers should consider two structural reforms: First, SEBI and the Ministry of Finance should evaluate a differentiated STT framework that provides tax offsets or reduced turnover levies for verified market-neutral paired trades executed under approved algorithmic risk-management protocols. Similar differential tax treatment already exists in other international jurisdictions where market-making and liquidity-providing transactions receive tax relief. Second, regulatory authorities should undertake a comprehensive revitalization of the Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) framework—streamlining custodian margin clearing, standardizing open recall tenures, and clarifying tax withholding rules—to enable genuine cash market shorting and reduce institutional over-reliance on futures markets.


\subsubsection*{Implications for Institutional Asset Managers and Quantitative Funds}
For chief investment officers, portfolio managers, and risk heads at Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), Portfolio Management Services (PMS), and mutual funds, our results provide an unambiguous managerial takeaway: Standalone statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock derivatives is economically unviable as an independent alpha product under prevailing tax laws.

Crucially, however, our findings do not imply that statistical arbitrage signals lack economic value. Rather, institutional decision makers must shift their operational paradigm from 'standalone alpha generation' to 'execution timing optimization.' In our secondary empirical tests, we evaluate whether Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residual signals can be harnessed as an execution overlay for long-only institutional portfolios. When an institutional mutual fund manager receives an order to accumulate an equity position over a 5-day horizon, conditioning execution timing on the s-score (delaying purchases when $s_t$ > 0 and accelerating orders when $s_t$ < -1.25) reduces implementation shortfall by an average of 28 to 44 basis points per execution cycle.

Because the institutional fund was already committed to executing the underlying trade, the execution overlay incurs zero incremental turnover tax, transforming an otherwise unprofitable trading strategy into a high-value cost-reduction tool for institutional investors. Portfolio managers can thereby capture the economic reality of mean reversion without triggering the destructive friction of standalone strategy turnover.

In summary, while statistical arbitrage cannot survive as an independent trading book in Indian single-stock F\&O, its underlying quantitative signals provide immense structural value for institutional execution and market microstructure analysis.
