\section{Conclusion and Practical Takeaways}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper provides a 16-year empirical look at statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock derivatives across distance pairs, PCA factor residuals, and machine learning models.

Four practical takeaways stand out:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Statistical Signals vs.\ Trading Reality:} While mean-reverting signals exist in Indian stocks with strong statistical confidence ($t > 4.0$), the typical spread deviation is smaller than the cost of entering and exiting the trade.
    \item \textbf{The Tax Barrier:} Mandatory Indian taxes (STT, stamp duty, GST, turnover charges) along with modest bid-ask slippage impose 12.0 to 18.5 basis points of friction per completed pairs cycle. For active daily strategies, this creates an annual drag exceeding 18\%, turning theoretical gains into severe net losses.
    \item \textbf{The Machine Learning Trade-Off:} Machine learning models do a better job of identifying short-term price moves on paper, but they increase portfolio turnover by 34\%. This extra churn creates even heavier net losses, showing that algorithmic complexity cannot overcome fixed statutory friction \citep{novymarx2016taxonomy}.
    \item \textbf{Value as an Execution Timing Tool:} Even though statistical arbitrage cannot work as an independent profit center in Indian single-stock F\&O, the residual signals are genuinely useful for execution desks. Portfolio managers who already need to rebalance long positions can use Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residuals to time their orders, potentially saving 20 to 40 basis points in execution costs.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, SEBI's October 2024 increase in Securities Transaction Tax raises the cost bar even higher for quantitative market-neutral strategies. As statutory friction grows, high-turnover arbitrage capital inevitably pulls back, with potential implications for relative price efficiency across Indian derivative markets.
