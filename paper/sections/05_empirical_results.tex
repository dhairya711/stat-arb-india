In theoretical frictionless conditions ($0.0\times$ cost multiplier), all three quantitative models demonstrate clear profitability. Strategy B (PCA-OU reversion) records a gross annualized return of $+17.91\%$ with a Sharpe ratio of $+1.74$ and a Newey--West $t$-statistic of $+7.49$ ($p < 10^{-12}$). Strategy A (distance pairs) achieves $+9.50\%$ gross annualized return with a Sharpe ratio of $+0.95$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.11$, $p < 0.001$). Strategy C (walk-forward machine learning) delivers $+8.75\%$ gross return with a Sharpe ratio of $+1.00$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.05$, $p < 0.001$).

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}, however, incorporating institutional transaction friction alters the results completely. Net annualized Sharpe ratios collapse to $-2.52$ for Strategy A, $-1.63$ for Strategy B, and $-2.96$ for Strategy C. All net Sharpe values are statistically negative with Newey--West $t$-statistics between $-7.00$ and $-12.00$ ($p < 10^{-11}$).

The drawdown underwater curves in Figure~\ref{fig:fig2} demonstrate this structural erosion. Gross peak-to-trough drawdowns stay modest between $-12.3\%$ and $-14.6\%$, whereas net portfolios suffer steady capital decay, accumulating drawdowns beyond $-95\%$ over the 16-year window.

Figure~\ref{fig:fig3} tracks rolling 36-month net Sharpe ratios. Net Sharpe performance remains negative across almost all rolling periods, illustrating that strategy losses are not artifactual byproducts of specific bear markets or isolated regimes, but a persistent feature of high-turnover trading under statutory frictions.

Two patterns in the sub-period data warrant attention:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Factor Neutrality and Crisis Stability:} During the February--March 2020 COVID-19 shock, Strategy B limited net annualized losses to $-0.56\%$ (Sharpe $-0.06$), noticeably preserving capital relative to Strategy A ($-57.45\%$) and Strategy C ($-69.86\%$). Filtering out systematic factors via PCA helped insulate Strategy B from broad correlation breakdowns that triggered divergence in naive pairs.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to Regulatory Taxes:} Following the October 1, 2024 increase in futures STT from 1.25 bps to 2.00 bps, net annual losses expanded across the board. Strategy A's net return declined to $-31.30\%$ (Sharpe $-3.19$), and Strategy B's net return worsened from $-12.97\%$ in SP2 to $-21.40\%$ in the post-hike window (Sharpe $-2.05$). This deterioration illustrates how statutory fee increases directly compress quantitative margins.
\end{enumerate}

Solving for the zero-Sharpe crossing $\text{SR}(M) = 0$ yields the following breakeven multipliers:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{breakeven}} = \begin{cases}
        0.48\times & \text{(equivalent to } 7.9 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy A)} \\
        0.90\times & \text{(equivalent to } 14.9 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy B)} \\
        0.44\times & \text{(equivalent to } 7.3 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy C)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Even under an aggressive 50\% cost reduction ($0.5\times$ multiplier, or $\sim 8.5$ bps round-trip), Strategies A and C remain unprofitable ($-7.83\%$ and $-8.58\%$ annualized). Strategy B reaches near-breakeven ($+0.58\%$ return, Sharpe $+0.06$). Because fixed statutory taxes account for roughly 4.0 to 5.0 bps per round-trip trade, institutional execution cannot realistically be compressed below $0.50\times$, precluding standalone deployment.

These results confirm that negative net returns are driven by execution drag rather than unhedged market exposure.
