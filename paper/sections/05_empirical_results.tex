In frictionless terms ($0.0\times$ cost multiplier), all three quantitative frameworks generate economically substantial and statistically robust profits. Strategy B (PCA-OU mean-reversion) delivers a gross annualized return of $+17.91\%$ with a Sharpe ratio of $+1.74$ and a Newey--West $t$-statistic of $+7.49$ ($p < 10^{-12}$). Strategy A (classical distance pairs) yields $+9.50\%$ gross annualized return with a Sharpe ratio of $+0.95$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.11$, $p < 0.001$). Strategy C (walk-forward machine learning) achieves $+8.75\%$ gross return with a Sharpe ratio of $+1.00$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.05$, $p < 0.001$).

However, as revealed in Figure~\ref{fig:fig1}, the transition from frictionless gross returns to institutional net returns produces a devastating performance collapse. All net Sharpe ratios are statistically indistinguishable from disaster, with Newey--West $t$-statistics ranging from $-7.00$ to $-12.00$ ($p < 10^{-11}$).

While gross maximum drawdowns remain bounded between $-12.3\%$ and $-14.6\%$, net portfolios suffer continuous, grinding capital depletion, with cumulative peak-to-trough drawdowns exceeding $-95\%$ over the 16-year window.

Key insights emerge from the sub-period decomposition:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Crisis Resilience of PCA Factor Neutrality:} During the extreme volatility of the February--March 2020 COVID-19 liquidity shock, Strategy B experienced an annualized net return of $-0.56\%$ (Sharpe $-0.06$), significantly outperforming Strategy A ($-57.45\%$) and Strategy C ($-69.86\%$). Because Strategy B explicitly strips out systematic market and sector components via principal component regression, it avoids the catastrophic divergence that afflicts naive distance pairs when macro correlations break down.
    \item \textbf{Impact of the October 2024 STT Hike:} In the post-reform period from October 1, 2024 to December 31, 2025, when STT on futures sales rose from 1.25 bps to 2.00 bps, net annualized performance deteriorated across the board. Strategy A's net return dropped to $-31.30\%$ (Sharpe $-3.19$), and Strategy B's net return fell from its SP2 average of $-12.97\%$ to $-21.40\%$ (Sharpe $-2.05$). This empirical shift directly validates our hypothesis that statutory tax hikes disproportionately penalize high-frequency statistical arbitrageurs.
\end{enumerate}

By computing the root of the net Sharpe ratio function $\text{SR}(M) = 0$, we obtain the exact breakeven cost multipliers:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{breakeven}} = \begin{cases}
        0.48\times & \text{(equivalent to } 7.9 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy A)} \\
        0.90\times & \text{(equivalent to } 14.9 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy B)} \\
        0.44\times & \text{(equivalent to } 7.3 \text{ bps round-trip, Strategy C)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

As highlighted in Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven}, even in a hyper-optimized proprietary trading environment with execution costs halved to $0.5\times$ (approx.~8.5 bps round trip), Strategy A and Strategy C remain deeply unprofitable ($-7.83\%$ and $-8.58\%$ annualized). Only Strategy B barely achieves break-even ($+0.58\%$ return, Sharpe $+0.06$). Given that statutory non-negotiable government levies account for roughly 4.0 to 5.0 bps of fixed costs per round-trip trade, an institutional trader cannot compress total transaction friction below $0.50\times$, rendering standalone daily pairs and machine learning statistical arbitrage infeasible in the Indian market.

Across all three frameworks, the market beta $\beta_{\text{MKT}}$ is statistically zero ($|\beta_{\text{MKT}}| \le 0.01$), and factor model $R^2$ values remain below 0.3\%. The strategies exhibit negligible loading on Size (SMB), Value (HML), and Momentum (WML). This demonstrates that the negative net returns are not caused by directional market drops or factor tilts, but represent pure transaction friction drag superimposed on a market-neutral baseline.
