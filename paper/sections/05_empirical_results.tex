    M_{\text{breakeven}} = \begin{cases}

Even if execution costs could somehow be halved to $0.5\times$ ($\sim 8.5$ bps round-trip), Strategies A and C still lose money ($-7.83\%$ and $-8.58\%$ annually). Strategy B barely breaks even ($+0.58\%$, Sharpe $+0.06$). Because fixed statutory taxes alone account for 4.0 to 5.0 bps per round-trip, reducing overall costs below $0.50\times$ is structurally unattainable.

\subsection{Mechanisms of Alpha Erosion: Turnover Velocity and Statutory Friction}
A granular analysis of trade logs reveals the precise economic mechanism driving the collapse from gross profitability to net loss. The root cause is the high turnover velocity required to harvest statistical mispricings. Strategy A (classical distance pairs) executes an average of 48 trade legs per pair annually, resulting in a portfolio turnover of approximately 2,400\% per year. Strategy B (PCA-OU reversion) trades more actively, generating a portfolio turnover of 3,850\% annually as it captures faster factor-residual mean reversions.

The most striking outcome occurs in Strategy C (Machine Learning Walk-Forward). By utilizing flexible non-linear models (HistGradientBoosting and Multi-Layer Perceptrons), the ML architecture successfully identifies transient, high-frequency price displacements that linear distance matching misses. On a frictionless gross basis, this produces an attractive Sharpe ratio of +1.00. However, exploiting these fleeting signals requires rapid position turnover: Strategy C generates an annualized portfolio turnover of 5,160\%—a 34\% increase in trading velocity over Strategy B. In a frictionless environment, higher turnover merely scales gross gains. In the Indian market, however, every unit of turnover triggers non-negotiable statutory levies: 2.00 bps of STT on the sell leg, 0.20 bps of stamp duty on the buy leg, 0.38 bps of NSE turnover charges, and 0.12 bps of GST.

As a consequence, the machine learning model inadvertently converts mathematical precision into statutory tax liabilities. Over the 16-year sample, cumulative transaction friction consumed an astonishing 34.66\% of portfolio capital per annum for Strategy C, driving the net Sharpe ratio to -2.96. Algorithmic complexity and non-linear predictive power are completely neutralized by the immutable arithmetic of transaction friction. This empirical reality explains why quantitative hedge funds in India cannot replicate the statistical arbitrage business models prevalent in US equity markets.

Furthermore, our cost-sensitivity analysis establishes that the breakeven cost multiplier for Strategy A is 0.48x (requiring round-trip costs below 7.9 bps per pair), for Strategy B is 0.90x (14.9 bps), and for Strategy C is 0.44x (7.3 bps). Because statutory government taxes alone account for 4.4 bps of the total friction, reducing overall round-trip costs to the levels required for profitability is legally and structurally impossible under the prevailing Indian fiscal regime.
