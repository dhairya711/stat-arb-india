\section{Empirical Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Performance Summary and the Gross-Net Wedge}
Table~\ref{tab:table1} reports the full-sample annualized performance metrics for the baseline specifications of Strategies A, B, and C over the 2010--2025 sample period.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Full-Sample Annualized Strategy Performance (2010--2025)}
\label{tab:table1}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Gross Return} & \textbf{Net Return} & \textbf{Gross Sharpe} & \textbf{Net Sharpe} & \textbf{Net NW $t$-stat} & \textbf{Bootstrap 95\% CI (Sharpe)} \\
\midrule
Strategy A (Gatev Pairs)      & +9.50\%  & -25.16\% & +0.95 & -2.52 & -10.89 & [-2.96, -2.05] \\
Strategy B (PCA-OU Reversion) & +17.91\% & -16.75\% & +1.74 & -1.63 &  -7.00 & [-2.06, -1.17] \\
Strategy C (ML Walk-Forward)  & +8.75\%  & -25.91\% & +1.00 & -2.96 & -12.00 & [-3.51, -2.42] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

In frictionless terms ($0.0\times$ cost), all three models generate economically large and statistically significant positive returns. Strategy B yields a gross annualized return of $17.91\%$ with a Sharpe ratio of $1.74$ and a Newey-West $t$-statistic of $+7.49$. Strategy A achieves a gross Sharpe ratio of $0.95$ ($t$-stat $= +4.11$), and Strategy C achieves $1.00$ ($t$-stat $= +4.05$).

However, under baseline institutional costs ($1.0\times$), net returns are universally negative. Strategy A suffers a net loss of $-25.16\%$ per annum (Net Sharpe $=-2.52$, $t$-stat $=-10.89$). Strategy B drops to $-16.75\%$ (Net Sharpe $=-1.63$, $t$-stat $=-7.00$). Strategy C drops to $-25.91\%$ (Net Sharpe $=-2.96$, $t$-stat $=-12.00$). The stationary bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals for the net Sharpe ratio exclude zero in all specifications, establishing that the negative net performance is statistically robust and not driven by sample outliers.

The breakeven cost multipliers solve to:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{breakeven}} = \begin{cases}
        0.48\times \text{ (7.9 bps round-trip, Strategy A)} \\
        0.90\times \text{ (14.9 bps round-trip, Strategy B)} \\
        0.44\times \text{ (7.3 bps round-trip, Strategy C)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
As depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:breakeven}, even under a favorable proprietary trading desk environment with transaction costs cut in half ($0.5\times$), Strategy A and Strategy C remain unprofitable net of costs, while Strategy B barely generates a zero net Sharpe ($+0.06$).
