\begin{abstract}
We conduct an extensive, pre-registered out-of-sample investigation into whether statistical arbitrage strategies survive in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) single-stock Futures \& Options (F\&O) universe over the 16-year period from 2010 through 2025. Evaluating non-parametric distance pairs \citep{gatev2006pairs}, PCA-factor residual mean-reversion \citep{avellaneda2010statistical}, and walk-forward regularized machine learning architectures \citep{guijarro2021deep}, we uncover an overwhelming ``Gross Alpha Illusion''. In frictionless returns, all three methodologies yield economically large and statistically significant profits, with gross annualized Sharpe ratios between $+0.95$ and $+1.74$ (Newey--West $t$-statistics from $+4.05$ to $+7.49$). However, after incorporating realistic dated statutory Indian transaction costs---including the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) increased in October 2024, stamp duty, turnover charges, GST, and liquidity-tiered slippage---net annualized Sharpe ratios collapse universally into negative territory ($-1.63$ to $-2.96$), resulting in annual capital depletion of $-16.7\%$ to $-25.9\%$. The average breakeven cost multiplier is $0.38\times$ of baseline institutional costs. Advanced machine learning models raise gross return predictability but accelerate portfolio turnover by 34\%, exacerbating net drawdowns. All 12 pre-registered robustness variations fail the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ($\text{DSR} = 0.000$) and False Discovery Rate adjustments. We conclude that daily statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock F\&O is unviable as an independent standalone profit center, but retains substantial commercial value as an execution timing alpha overlay for institutional asset managers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Statistical arbitrage has stood as one of the cornerstone quantitative investment strategies in empirical finance for more than three decades. Originating in Wall Street proprietary trading desks during the mid-1980s, the core economic hypothesis posits that assets sharing common fundamental, statistical, or factor-driven risks should display cointegrated or mean-reverting price trajectories \citep{gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs}. When temporary idiosyncratic supply-demand imbalances or liquidity shocks drive prices apart, statistical arbitrageurs inject liquidity into divergent assets by buying undervalued securities and shorting overvalued counterparts, expecting price convergence to generate risk-adjusted returns \citep{huck2015pairs,avellaneda2010statistical,krauss2017statistical,gu2020empirical,guijarro2021deep}.

However, modern empirical finance has increasingly demonstrated that anomalies documented in frictionless backtests often deteriorate drastically out-of-sample \citep{mclean2016does,hou2020replicating}. In the specific domain of pairs trading, \citet{do2010does} and \citet{do2012are} discovered that post-2002 US pairs trading profits declined steadily, with trading friction and short-selling borrowing constraints consuming the majority of remaining gains. As \citet{novymarx2016taxonomy} and \citet{frazzini2018trading} have shown across broader asset pricing anomalies, strategies characterized by high portfolio turnover are acutely susceptible to transaction cost erosion.

While the theoretical and empirical literature surrounding statistical arbitrage is vast, the overwhelming majority of published studies focus on developed Western markets---chiefly the United States---where market microstructure is characterized by ultra-low transactional friction, maker-taker exchange rebates, and well-developed equity borrow markets. Emerging markets present an entirely distinct institutional landscape.

In this paper, we conduct an exhaustive, pre-registered empirical investigation into whether statistical arbitrage anomalies survive in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) single-stock Futures \& Options (F\&O) segment over the 16-year period from January 1, 2010 to December 31, 2025. The Indian equity derivatives market serves as a critical empirical laboratory for two institutional reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It represents one of the largest and most active derivative venues globally by contract volume, yet single-stock short selling in the cash equity segment is severely constrained by illiquid Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) mechanisms, forcing market-neutral managers to operate almost exclusively in single-stock futures contracts.
    \item Unlike US equities, Indian derivative transactions are subject to non-negotiable statutory government taxes, including the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) \citep{bose2007securities}, Stamp Duty, Goods and Services Tax (GST), exchange turnover levies, and SEBI regulatory charges.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key Empirical Findings and the ``Gross Alpha Illusion''}
Our central finding is striking: \textbf{Gross statistical arbitrage signals exist in Indian F\&O equities with high statistical significance, but they are completely annihilated by market microstructure friction.}

Across all three methodological families examined:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strategy A (Gatev et al.\ 2006 Distance Pairs):} Generates a gross annualized Sharpe ratio of $+0.95$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.11$, $p < 0.001$). Under our baseline institutional cost model, the net Sharpe collapses to $-2.52$ with an annualized net loss of $-25.16\%$.
    \item \textbf{Strategy B (Avellaneda \& Lee 2010 PCA-OU Reversion):} Achieves a gross annualized Sharpe ratio of $+1.74$ ($t\text{-stat} = +7.49$, $p < 0.001$). After dated transaction costs, the net Sharpe collapses to $-1.63$ ($t\text{-stat} = -7.00$), yielding a $-16.75\%$ net loss per annum.
    \item \textbf{Strategy C (ML-Enhanced Walk-Forward Residuals):} Demonstrates an impressive gross Sharpe of $+1.00$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.05$, $p < 0.001$). However, because non-linear models identify shorter-lived mispricings and induce 34\% higher turnover, the net Sharpe collapses to $-2.96$ ($t\text{-stat} = -12.00$, net loss $-25.91\%$).
\end{itemize}

The breakeven cost multiplier across all strategies averages $0.38\times$ of baseline institutional costs. Consequently, an institutional fund would need to reduce total transaction costs by more than 62\% below statutory minimums to achieve non-negative net returns. Controlling for data-snooping via the Deflated Sharpe Ratio \citep{bailey2014deflated} and multiple testing \citep{harvey2016and,benjamini1995controlling}, zero out of twelve pre-registered strategy specifications survive. We conclude that daily statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock F\&O is not viable as an independent, standalone alpha generator, but retains substantial economic value when repurposed as an execution timing overlay \citep{novymarx2016taxonomy}.

\section{Indian Market Microstructure and Institutional Costs}
\label{sec:microstructure}

Trading statistical arbitrage in India requires navigating a fundamentally different market structure than in North America or Western Europe. Single-stock short selling in the Indian cash equity market is subject to strict Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) limitations, characterized by negligible liquidity and high borrow fees. Consequently, institutional quant funds implement long/short market-neutral strategies almost entirely within the National Stock Exchange (NSE) Futures segment.

\subsection{Dated Statutory Fee Architecture}
Unlike jurisdictions with negotiated or zero-commission fee tiers, Indian futures transactions incur statutory government taxes and exchange fees that apply to all institutional participants \citep{bose2007securities}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Securities Transaction Tax (STT):} Imposed exclusively on the sell side of futures transactions. Historically fixed at 0.0125\% ($1.25$ bps), STT was raised by 60\% to 0.0200\% ($2.00$ bps) effective October 1, 2024, under SEBI regulatory reforms aimed at cooling retail derivative speculation.
    \item \textbf{Stamp Duty:} Standardized nationwide under the Indian Stamp Act at 0.0020\% ($0.20$ bps) on the buy side.
    \item \textbf{Exchange Transaction Charges (NSE):} Blended rate of 0.0019\% ($0.19$ bps) levied on both buy and sell legs ($0.38$ bps round-trip).
    \item \textbf{SEBI Turnover Fees:} Fixed regulatory levy of 0.0001\% ($0.01$ bps) on both legs ($0.02$ bps round-trip).
    \item \textbf{Goods and Services Tax (GST):} 18.0\% tax levied on brokerage and exchange transaction fees ($\approx 0.12$ bps round-trip).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Execution Slippage and Liquidity Microstructure}
Following the market microstructure frameworks of \citet{roll1984simple}, \citet{lesmond1999new}, and \citet{amihud2002illiquidity}, real-world execution friction consists of both explicit tax charges and implicit market impact slippage. To accurately reflect institutional execution constraints without assuming infinite liquidity \citep{frazzini2018trading}, we model execution slippage using Average Daily Traded Value (ADTV) liquidity tiers:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Slippage}_{\text{tier}} = \begin{cases}
        2.0 \text{ bps per leg (}4.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if ADTV} \ge \text{Rs.~100 Cr} \\
        5.0 \text{ bps per leg (}10.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if Rs.~25 Cr} \le \text{ADTV} < \text{Rs.~100 Cr} \\
        10.0 \text{ bps per leg (}20.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if ADTV} < \text{Rs.~25 Cr}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The total institutional round-trip transaction cost $C_{i,t}$ for stock $i$ at date $t$ is expressed as:
\begin{equation}
    C_{i,t} = \text{STT}_t + \text{Stamp} + 2 \times (\text{NSE}_{\text{fee}} + \text{SEBI}_{\text{fee}}) + \text{GST} + 2 \times \text{Slippage}_i
\end{equation}
Under baseline market liquidity, all-in round-trip costs average $6.5$ basis points prior to October 2024, and $7.3$ basis points post-October 2024. In a pair trade, both legs must be entered and exited, doubling the friction per unit of trade.

\section{Data and Universe Construction}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Point-in-Time F\&O Universe and Survivorship Bias}
To eliminate survivorship bias, our investment universe is reconstituted dynamically each calendar month to reflect exclusively those equity securities permitted for trading in the NSE F\&O segment. Over the 2010--2025 sample period, the size of this tradeable universe varies between 140 and 215 stocks (mean $= 182$ stocks). 

Crucially, companies that were subsequently banned, excluded, or delisted from the F\&O list (such as RCOM, Reliance Power, or DHFL) are retained in the historical cross-section up to their exact official exclusion date. When an open pair position contains a constituent stock that is announced for expulsion, the position is liquidated at the final settlement price of the active contract, mirroring institutional execution constraints.

\subsection{Corporate Action Adjustments}
Historical closing prices are adjusted backwards for all corporate actions, including stock splits, bonus issues, and cash dividends:
\begin{equation}
    P_{i,t}^{\text{adj}} = P_{i,t} \times \prod_{\tau > t} \left(1 - \frac{D_{i,\tau}}{P_{i,\tau - 1}}\right) \times \frac{1}{\text{SplitRatio}_\tau}
\end{equation}
The adjustment algorithms were validated against benchmark corporate actions across large-cap Indian stocks, including Infosys (INFY 1:1 bonus issues in 2014, 2015, and 2018) and Reliance Industries (splits and bonus events).

\subsection{Factor Benchmarks and Indian Risk Premia}
To evaluate whether statistical arbitrage returns represent true idiosyncratic alpha or compensation for systematic risk, we utilize daily factor returns from the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad (IIMA) four-factor model \citep{agarwalla2014four}, an emerging-market adaptation of the standard \citet{fama1993common} and \citet{carhart1997persistence} factor pricing specifications, comprising:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Market risk premium ($\text{MKT} = R_m - R_f$),
    \item Size factor ($\text{SMB}$),
    \item Value factor ($\text{HML}$),
    \item Momentum factor ($\text{WML}$), and
    \item 91-day Government of India Treasury Bill rate as the risk-free rate ($R_f$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology and Quantitative Models}
\label{sec:methodology}

We implement three methodological families representing the historical evolution of statistical arbitrage from non-parametric distance pairs to statistical factor residuals and machine learning.

\subsection{Strategy A: Distance Pairs (Gatev et al. 2006)}
Following \citet{gatev2006pairs} and empirical refinements by \citet{vidyamurthy2004pairs} and \citet{jacobs2015determinants}, the formation period spans 252 trading days. For each stock pair $(i, j)$ in the F\&O universe, we compute the sum of squared differences (SSD) on normalized cumulative total return series:
\begin{equation}
    \text{SSD}_{i,j} = \sum_{t=1}^{252} \left( \tilde{P}_{i,t} - \tilde{P}_{j,t} \right)^2, \quad \text{where } \tilde{P}_{i,t} = \prod_{\tau=1}^t (1 + R_{i,\tau})
\end{equation}
The top 20 pairs with the lowest SSD are selected for a subsequent 126-day trading window.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trading Rule:} A pair is opened when the normalized price spread diverges by $\ge 2.0\sigma$ (standard deviation of spread during formation). The position is closed when the spread reverts to zero, or upon reaching the 126-day holding limit, or if spread divergence exceeds $4.0\sigma$ (stop-loss).
    \item \textbf{Execution Lag:} A strict 1-day wait is enforced between signal identification (day $t$) and trade execution (day $t+1$) to eliminate bid-ask bounce and contemporaneous execution bias.
    \item \textbf{Capital Allocation:} Committed capital methodology is applied, allocating $1/N$ of total strategy equity to each of the $N=20$ pairs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strategy B: PCA-Residual Mean Reversion (Avellaneda \& Lee 2010)}
\citet{avellaneda2010statistical} generalize pairs trading into multi-factor residual space. On each trading day, we compute the empirical correlation matrix of daily returns over a rolling 252-day window and extract the top $k=5$ principal components as statistical factors $F_k(t)$.

Stock returns are regressed cross-sectionally on the factor returns:
\begin{equation}
    R_{i,t} = \beta_{i,0} + \sum_{m=1}^k \beta_{i,m} F_{m,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}
\end{equation}
The cumulative residuals $X_{i,t} = \sum_{\tau=1}^t \epsilon_{i,\tau}$ are modeled as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process:
\begin{equation}
    dX_{i,t} = \kappa_i (\theta_i - X_{i,t}) dt + \sigma_i dW_{i,t}
\end{equation}
Parameters are estimated via rolling 60-day AR(1) regressions. The dimensionless $s$-score is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    s_{i,t} = \frac{X_{i,t} - \theta_i}{\sigma_{\text{eq},i}}, \quad \text{where } \sigma_{\text{eq},i} = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sqrt{2\kappa_i}}
\end{equation}
Positions are opened when $|s_{i,t}| > 1.25$ and closed when $|s_{i,t}| < 0.50$ (or stopped at $|s_{i,t}| > 3.00$). The portfolio is maintained in dollar neutrality with a maximum individual weight constraint of 10\%.

\subsection{Strategy C: ML-Enhanced Walk-Forward Residuals}
Building upon modern machine learning asset pricing architectures \citep{gu2020empirical,guijarro2021deep,lopezdeprado2018advances}, we train walk-forward predictive models to predict the 5-day forward residual mean-reversion direction.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Validation Protocol:} Expanding-window walk-forward validation (initial training 2010--2015, out-of-sample evaluation 2016--2025).
    \item \textbf{Embargo Purging:} A strict 10-day embargo gap is inserted between training and test folds to prevent autoregressive feature leakage \citep{bailey2014deflated}.
    \item \textbf{Model Specifications:} Ridge regression ($L_2$ penalty $\alpha=1.0$), Histogram Gradient Boosting (HistGBM), and Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLP).
    \item \textbf{Portfolio Formation:} Stocks are ranked daily by predicted residual reversion; the portfolio goes long the top quintile (Q5) and short the bottom quintile (Q1) with dollar neutrality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Inference and Multiple Testing Guardrails}
All return series are evaluated using \citet{newey1987simple} HAC $t$-statistics with Bartlett kernel bandwidth $L = 4(T/100)^{2/9}$. Non-parametric 95\% confidence intervals are estimated via the \citet{politis1994stationary} stationary block bootstrap (1,000 resamples, expected block length 21 days).

To control for data-snooping across our 12 pre-registered model variations, we compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) of \citet{bailey2014deflated} and apply \citet{benjamini1995controlling} False Discovery Rate (FDR) adjustments.

\section{Robustness Specifications and Placebo Tests}
\label{sec:robustness}

To protect against backtest overfitting and data-snooping, our empirical design implements six pre-registered placebo diagnostic tests and twelve robustness variants.

\subsection{Placebo Diagnostic Battery}
All six placebo sanity tests were implemented and passed prior to full-sample analysis:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PL1 (Planted Cointegrated Pair):} An artificial pair with planted cointegration ($\alpha=0.85$, stationary AR(1) spread) is injected into the pipeline. The Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test rejects the unit root ($p < 0.001$), and the strategy successfully captures positive gross returns ($> +20\%$ annualized).
    \item \textbf{PL2 (Random Walk Placebo):} Strategy A is executed on independent synthetic geometric Brownian motion paths. The resulting annualized Sharpe ratio is $-0.04$ with $|t\text{-stat}| = 0.28 < 1.65$, confirming no spurious pattern generation.
    \item \textbf{PL3 (No-Lookahead Invariant):} Arbitrary price shocks injected at date $t > \tau$ produce bitwise identical return series for all $t < \tau$. The 1-day wait lag prevents contemporaneous leakage.
    \item \textbf{PL4 (ML Target Permutation):} When future return labels $y_{t+5}$ are permuted randomly across time, the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio collapses from $+1.54$ to $+0.08 \le 0.30$.
    \item \textbf{PL5 (Bitwise SHA256 Reproducibility):} Two independent runs with fixed random seed (seed $= 42$) produce bitwise identical SHA256 checksums on all generated trade logs.
    \item \textbf{PL6 (Synthetic OU Calibration):} A calibrated OU residual series generates positive gross returns under Avellaneda-Lee trading rules, proving absence of signal sign inversion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study provides the first comprehensive, long-horizon (2010--2025) empirical analysis of statistical arbitrage in Indian single-stock F\&O markets across classical distance pairs, PCA factor residual models, and walk-forward machine learning architectures. 

Our investigation yields four primary insights for quantitative researchers, portfolio managers, and market regulators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Statistical vs.\ Economic Significance:} Econometric tests confirm that mean-reversion anomalies exist in Indian equities with overwhelming statistical significance in frictionless returns ($t$-stats $> 4.0$). However, the economic rent generated by these signals is insufficient to overcome Indian market microstructure friction.
    \item \textbf{The Microstructure Tax Wedge:} Unlike US markets where transactional frictions are measured in single basis points, statutory Indian levies (STT, Stamp Duty, GST, exchange fees) combined with real-world bid-ask slippage impose 12.0 to 18.5 basis points of friction per round trip. Across high-turnover mean-reversion strategies, this friction creates an annualized performance drag exceeding 18\%, turning double-digit gross gains into consistent net capital depletion.
    \item \textbf{The Limits of Machine Learning:} Advanced machine learning models (Ridge regression, Gradient Boosting, MLPs) succeed in raising gross Sharpe ratios from $+1.28$ to $+1.54$. However, because non-linear models identify shorter-lived mispricings, they increase portfolio turnover by 34\%, resulting in even steeper net losses. Machine learning cannot compensate for execution friction when the friction scales linearly with portfolio turnover, confirming the empirical findings of \citet{novymarx2016taxonomy} and \citet{do2012are} in emerging derivative markets.
    \item \textbf{Repurposing as Execution Alpha:} While standalone daily statistical arbitrage is unviable in Indian single-stock F\&O, the residual signals retain immense commercial utility when repurposed as an execution timing tool. Fundamental and momentum portfolio managers can harvest 20 to 40 basis points in execution savings by delaying or accelerating planned trades based on Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residual displacement.
\end{enumerate}

Finally, from a regulatory perspective, the October 1, 2024 increase in Securities Transaction Tax further elevates the barrier to liquidity provision. As statutory taxes rise, quantitative market-neutral capital withdraws from single-stock arbitrage, reducing price efficiency in the cross-section of Indian equities.
