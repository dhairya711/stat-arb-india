\begin{abstract}
We examine whether statistical arbitrage strategies generate viable net excess returns in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) single-stock Futures \& Options (F\&O) market over a 16-year window from 2010 to 2025. Across three established quantitative methodologies---non-parametric distance pairs \citep{gatev2006pairs}, PCA-based residual mean-reversion \citep{avellaneda2010statistical}, and regularized walk-forward machine learning \citep{guijarro2021deep}---we identify a stark divergence between gross predictability and net execution performance. In theoretical, frictionless conditions, all three strategies yield positive, statistically significant gross returns, achieving gross annualized Sharpe ratios between $+0.95$ and $+1.74$ with Newey--West $t$-statistics from $+4.05$ to $+7.49$. Once actual dated statutory transaction costs (Securities Transaction Tax, stamp duty, exchange levies, GST) and realistic liquidity-tiered bid-ask slippage are subtracted, net performance turns negative across all specifications. Net annualized Sharpe ratios drop to between $-1.63$ and $-2.96$, corresponding to annual net portfolio losses of $-16.7\%$ to $-25.9\%$. The estimated breakeven transaction friction is $0.38\times$ baseline institutional costs. Machine learning models succeed in capturing shorter-lived mean-reversion signals but trigger a 34\% increase in rebalancing turnover, aggravating drag from trading friction. Across 12 pre-registered model variations, none achieve positive net significance after accounting for multiple testing and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio ($\text{DSR} = 0.000$). Our empirical findings indicate that standalone daily statistical arbitrage cannot overcome statutory frictions in Indian single-stock derivatives, though the underlying price-displacement signals provide actionable utility as execution-timing overlays for institutional rebalancing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantitative relative-value trading has long occupied a prominent position in empirical finance. The fundamental premise of statistical arbitrage rests on law-of-one-price dynamics: securities tied to shared fundamental, statistical, or factor risk profiles should exhibit co-moving, mean-reverting price spreads over intermediate horizons \citep{gatev2006pairs,vidyamurthy2004pairs}. When temporary supply imbalances, institutional flow pressures, or inventory dislocations push these spreads away from equilibrium, quantitative managers step in as liquidity providers, buying underpriced assets and shorting overpriced counterparts in anticipation of convergence \citep{avellaneda2010statistical,krauss2017statistical,gu2020empirical}.

Over the past two decades, however, empirical asset pricing has revealed that anomalies identified in frictionless simulations frequently decay or disappear out of sample \citep{mclean2016does,hou2020replicating}. In pairs trading specifically, \citet{do2010does} and \citet{do2012are} documented a steady downward trend in US profitability after 2002, with short-sale borrowing fees and execution friction capturing the majority of theoretical gains. More broadly, \citet{novymarx2016taxonomy} and \citet{frazzini2018trading} demonstrated that high-turnover anomaly strategies face severe decay once practical market microstructure constraints are accounted for.

The vast majority of existing statistical arbitrage literature examines developed equity venues, most notably the United States. In those markets, trading friction is minimal, maker-taker rebates are common, and borrow markets are deep. Emerging equity derivative venues, by contrast, present a different institutional profile.

This paper provides an empirical evaluation of statistical arbitrage in the National Stock Exchange of India (NSE) single-stock Futures \& Options (F\&O) market over the 16-year period from January 2010 through December 2025. Two specific institutional features make the Indian derivative landscape particularly informative for studying implementation friction:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Cash equity short selling in India is constrained by a thin Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) framework. As a practical consequence, quantitative fund managers seeking two-sided market neutrality execute short exposure primarily through single-stock futures contracts.
    \item Unlike US venues where transaction fees are predominantly negotiated, Indian derivative transactions bear mandatory statutory taxes and levies, including the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) \citep{bose2007securities}, state stamp duty, GST, exchange charges, and regulatory turnover fees.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Empirical Findings: Signal Predictability vs.\ Implementation Drag}
Our empirical results demonstrate a clear tension: \textbf{statistically robust mean-reverting signals exist in the cross-section of Indian single-stock derivatives, but statutory tax wedges and execution friction entirely eliminate net portfolio gains.}

Specifically, across the three strategy families tested:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Strategy A (Gatev et al.\ 2006 Distance Pairs):} Generates a gross annualized Sharpe ratio of $+0.95$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.11$, $p < 0.001$). Under baseline institutional transaction costs, net performance deteriorates to a Sharpe ratio of $-2.52$, producing an annualized loss of $-25.16\%$.
    \item \textbf{Strategy B (Avellaneda \& Lee 2010 PCA-OU Reversion):} Delivers a gross Sharpe ratio of $+1.74$ ($t\text{-stat} = +7.49$, $p < 0.001$). Accounting for dated statutory transaction costs, the net Sharpe drops to $-1.63$ ($t\text{-stat} = -7.00$), corresponding to a $-16.75\%$ annual net loss.
    \item \textbf{Strategy C (Walk-Forward Machine Learning Residuals):} Yields a gross Sharpe ratio of $+1.00$ ($t\text{-stat} = +4.05$, $p < 0.001$). Because non-linear predictive models target shorter-lived dislocations, portfolio turnover increases by 34\%, driving the net Sharpe ratio down to $-2.96$ ($t\text{-stat} = -12.00$, annual net loss of $-25.91\%$).
\end{itemize}

Across all models, the breakeven cost multiplier averages $0.38\times$ baseline institutional costs. Achieving break-even would require an institutional investor to compress total trading friction by over 62\% relative to statutory minimums. When adjusting for multiple hypothesis testing and backtest data-mining via the Deflated Sharpe Ratio \citep{bailey2014deflated,harvey2016and,benjamini1995controlling}, zero out of twelve pre-registered model variations produce statistically positive net returns. We conclude that while daily statistical arbitrage cannot function as an independent standalone profit strategy in Indian single-stock F\&O, these mean-reverting residual signals offer meaningful practical value as execution-timing overlays for long-only or institutional rebalancing \citep{novymarx2016taxonomy}.

\section{Institutional Framework and Transaction Cost Architecture}
\label{sec:microstructure}

Implementing statistical arbitrage in India requires operating under institutional constraints that differ substantially from developed markets. Cash equity shorting is hindered by low participation in the formal Securities Lending and Borrowing mechanism, making single-stock futures contracts the primary route for short legs in market-neutral portfolios.

\subsection{Statutory Indian Derivative Fee Schedule}
Unlike jurisdictions with zero-commission retail trading or negotiated maker-taker models, transactions in Indian single-stock futures face mandatory government taxes and exchange fees that apply across all institutional market participants \citep{bose2007securities}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Securities Transaction Tax (STT):} Charged exclusively on the sell leg of futures transactions. Historically set at 0.0125\% ($1.25$ bps), STT was raised by 60\% to 0.0200\% ($2.00$ bps) effective October 1, 2024, as part of SEBI measures to moderate derivative speculative volume.
    \item \textbf{Stamp Duty:} Applied uniformly across states under the Indian Stamp Act at 0.0020\% ($0.20$ bps) on buy transactions.
    \item \textbf{Exchange Turnover Charges (NSE):} Blended fee of 0.0019\% ($0.19$ bps) assessed on both entry and exit legs ($0.38$ bps round-trip).
    \item \textbf{SEBI Regulatory Fees:} Standard regulatory turnover fee of 0.0001\% ($0.01$ bps) on each leg ($0.02$ bps round-trip).
    \item \textbf{Goods and Services Tax (GST):} 18.0\% tax levied on brokerage and exchange transaction charges ($\approx 0.12$ bps round-trip).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Liquidity-Tiered Slippage and Total Friction Model}
Following established microstructure literature \citep{roll1984simple,lesmond1999new,amihud2002illiquidity,frazzini2018trading}, execution friction includes both statutory taxes and bid-ask spread impact. To model institutional order execution realistically without assuming limitless market depth, we assign execution slippage across Average Daily Traded Value (ADTV) tiers:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Slippage}_{\text{tier}} = \begin{cases}
        2.0 \text{ bps per leg (}4.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if ADTV} \ge \text{Rs.~100 Cr} \\
        5.0 \text{ bps per leg (}10.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if Rs.~25 Cr} \le \text{ADTV} < \text{Rs.~100 Cr} \\
        10.0 \text{ bps per leg (}20.0\text{ bps round-trip)} & \text{if ADTV} < \text{Rs.~25 Cr}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

The total institutional round-trip transaction cost $C_{i,t}$ for stock $i$ on date $t$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    C_{i,t} = \text{STT}_t + \text{Stamp} + 2 \times (\text{NSE}_{\text{fee}} + \text{SEBI}_{\text{fee}}) + \text{GST} + 2 \times \text{Slippage}_i
\end{equation}
For a typical large-cap derivative stock, round-trip costs averaged approximately $6.5$ basis points prior to October 2024, and $7.3$ basis points following the STT increase. Because pairs and factor-residual portfolios involve two simultaneous legs, entry and exit require four single-stock transactions, doubling the aggregate drag per trade cycle.

\section{Data and Universe Construction}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Point-in-Time F\&O Universe and Survivorship Control}
To prevent survivorship bias, our investment universe is dynamically reconstituted on a monthly basis to include only those individual stocks officially approved by the NSE for single-stock F\&O trading. Between 2010 and 2025, the eligible tradeable cross-section ranged from 140 to 215 stocks, with an average size of 182 securities.

Companies subsequently removed, suspended, or delisted from the F\&O eligible list (such as Reliance Communications, Reliance Power, or DHFL) are retained in the empirical dataset up to their exact official exclusion date. Any active strategy positions in an excluded stock are closed at the final settlement price of the expiring contract, reflecting standard institutional portfolio rules.

\subsection{Corporate Action Handling}
Historical price and return series are adjusted for corporate events, including stock splits, bonus allocations, and cash dividend payouts:
\begin{equation}
    P_{i,t}^{\text{adj}} = P_{i,t} \times \prod_{\tau > t} \left(1 - \frac{D_{i,\tau}}{P_{i,\tau - 1}}\right) \times \frac{1}{\text{SplitRatio}_\tau}
\end{equation}
Our corporate adjustment procedure was cross-checked against documented historical corporate actions in large Indian equities, including bonus distributions in Infosys (2014, 2015, and 2018) and capital restructuring in Reliance Industries.

\subsection{Asset Pricing Factor Benchmarks}
To determine whether strategy performance represents genuine idiosyncratic alpha rather than unintended exposures to known market factors, we benchmark strategy returns against the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad (IIMA) four-factor model \citep{agarwalla2014four}, an emerging-market implementation of the \citet{fama1993common} and \citet{carhart1997persistence} factor structures:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Market risk premium ($\text{MKT} = R_m - R_f$),
    \item Size factor ($\text{SMB}$),
    \item Value factor ($\text{HML}$),
    \item Momentum factor ($\text{WML}$), and
    \item 91-day Government of India Treasury Bill rate ($R_f$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Empirical Methodology and Model Design}
\label{sec:methodology}

We evaluate three methodological approaches representing key milestones in the development of statistical arbitrage: classical distance pairs, factor-residual Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, and machine learning models.

\subsection{Strategy A: Non-Parametric Distance Pairs}
Following the framework of \citet{gatev2006pairs} with standard modifications \citep{vidyamurthy2004pairs,jacobs2015determinants}, each pair is formed using a 252-day formation window. For all eligible stock pairs $(i, j)$ in the F\&O universe, we calculate the sum of squared differences (SSD) on normalized cumulative return series:
\begin{equation}
    \text{SSD}_{i,j} = \sum_{t=1}^{252} \left( \tilde{P}_{i,t} - \tilde{P}_{j,t} \right)^2, \quad \text{where } \tilde{P}_{i,t} = \prod_{\tau=1}^t (1 + R_{i,\tau})
\end{equation}
The 20 pairs with the lowest SSD values are tracked during a subsequent 126-day trading window.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Trading Rule:} A position is opened when the normalized price spread deviates by $\ge 2.0\sigma$ (using formation-period spread standard deviation). Trades are exited upon spread convergence to zero, at the end of the 126-day window, or upon reaching a $4.0\sigma$ stop-loss threshold.
    \item \textbf{Execution Delay:} A 1-day wait is strictly enforced between signal generation (day $t$) and trade implementation (day $t+1$) to rule out bid-ask bounce and lookahead bias.
    \item \textbf{Capital Allocation:} Capital is committed equally across the $N=20$ monitored pairs ($1/N$ allocation).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Strategy B: PCA-Residual Mean Reversion}
Following \citet{avellaneda2010statistical}, individual stock returns are projected onto systematic statistical factors. Each trading day, principal component analysis is performed on the rolling 252-day empirical return correlation matrix to extract the top $k=5$ principal components $F_k(t)$.

Cross-sectional regressions relate stock returns to factor realizations:
\begin{equation}
    R_{i,t} = \beta_{i,0} + \sum_{m=1}^k \beta_{i,m} F_{m,t} + \epsilon_{i,t}
\end{equation}
Cumulative residuals $X_{i,t} = \sum_{\tau=1}^t \epsilon_{i,\tau}$ are modeled as a continuous-time Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process:
\begin{equation}
    dX_{i,t} = \kappa_i (\theta_i - X_{i,t}) dt + \sigma_i dW_{i,t}
\end{equation}
Model parameters are calibrated via rolling 60-day AR(1) regressions. The normalized $s$-score is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    s_{i,t} = \frac{X_{i,t} - \theta_i}{\sigma_{\text{eq},i}}, \quad \text{where } \sigma_{\text{eq},i} = \frac{\sigma_i}{\sqrt{2\kappa_i}}
\end{equation}
Positions are initiated when $|s_{i,t}| > 1.25$ and closed when $|s_{i,t}| < 0.50$ (or stopped at $|s_{i,t}| > 3.00$). The aggregate portfolio is rebalanced to dollar neutrality, subject to a 10\% cap on individual stock weights.

\subsection{Strategy C: Walk-Forward Machine Learning Residuals}
Extending quantitative residual models through machine learning \citep{gu2020empirical,guijarro2021deep,lopezdeprado2018advances}, we train walk-forward predictive algorithms to forecast 5-day forward residual mean-reversion directions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Walk-Forward Protocol:} Expanding-window walk-forward training is used, starting with 2010--2015 as the initial training set and evaluating out-of-sample performance over 2016--2025.
    \item \textbf{Embargo Gap:} A 10-day purging embargo separates training and test windows to prevent serial correlation leakage \citep{bailey2014deflated}.
    \item \textbf{Architectures Tested:} Ridge regression ($L_2$ regularization $\alpha=1.0$), Histogram Gradient Boosting (HistGBM), and Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLP).
    \item \textbf{Portfolio Construction:} Securities are ranked by predicted reversion scores; the portfolio takes long positions in the top quintile (Q5) and short positions in the bottom quintile (Q1), maintaining dollar neutrality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Statistical Inference and Multiple Testing Guardrails}
All performance series are evaluated using \citet{newey1987simple} heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) $t$-statistics with Bartlett kernel bandwidth $L = 4(T/100)^{2/9}$. In addition, 95\% confidence intervals are estimated via the \citet{politis1994stationary} stationary block bootstrap (1,000 resamples with an expected block length of 21 days).

To adjust for data-mining across our 12 pre-registered specifications, we compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) of \citet{bailey2014deflated} and apply \citet{benjamini1995controlling} False Discovery Rate (FDR) corrections.

\section{Robustness Analysis and Diagnostic Placebo Testing}
\label{sec:robustness}

To verify empirical validity and guard against backtest overfitting, our research design includes six pre-registered placebo diagnostic checks and twelve robustness specifications.

\subsection{Diagnostic Placebo Tests}
All six placebo diagnostics were executed and confirmed before finalizing the full-sample study:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PL1 (Planted Cointegrated Pair):} A synthetic pair with known cointegration ($\alpha=0.85$, stationary AR(1) spread) is injected into the data pipeline. Augmented Dickey-Fuller tests cleanly reject the unit root ($p < 0.001$), and the model captures positive gross returns ($> +20\%$ annualized).
    \item \textbf{PL2 (Random Walk Benchmark):} Strategy A is applied to independent geometric Brownian motion price paths. The resulting Sharpe ratio is $-0.04$ with $|t\text{-stat}| = 0.28 < 1.65$, confirming no spurious pattern generation on uncoupled assets.
    \item \textbf{PL3 (Lookahead Invariance Check):} Synthetic price shocks added at date $t > \tau$ generate bitwise identical return histories for all $t < \tau$. The 1-day execution lag prevents contemporaneous lookahead bias.
    \item \textbf{PL4 (Target Permutation Test):} Randomly permuting forward return targets $y_{t+5}$ across time causes the out-of-sample Sharpe ratio to drop from $+1.54$ to $+0.08 \le 0.30$.
    \item \textbf{PL5 (Bitwise SHA256 Reproducibility):} Independent repeated runs with a fixed seed (seed $= 42$) generate identical SHA256 hashes across all trade records.
    \item \textbf{PL6 (Synthetic OU Calibration):} A simulated mean-reverting OU series yields positive gross performance under Avellaneda-Lee trading rules, verifying proper signal polarity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Practical Implications}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study provides a 16-year empirical examination of statistical arbitrage in the Indian single-stock derivatives market across distance pairs, PCA factor residual models, and walk-forward machine learning algorithms.

Four key insights emerge for quantitative practitioners and market regulators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Predictability vs.\ Profitability:} While mean-reverting signals exist in Indian equities with statistical significance in frictionless returns ($t$-stats $> 4.0$), the magnitude of the underlying mispricings is smaller than the round-trip friction required to trade them.
    \item \textbf{Impact of Statutory Tax Wedges:} Mandatory Indian taxes and exchange levies (STT, stamp duty, GST, turnover fees) coupled with bid-ask slippage impose between 12.0 and 18.5 basis points of friction per completed pairs cycle. For strategies that trade frequently, this creates an annualized drag exceeding 18\%, turning theoretical gains into net capital losses.
    \item \textbf{Trade-Offs in Machine Learning:} Non-linear and machine learning models improve gross return predictability (raising gross Sharpe from $+1.28$ to $+1.54$), but identify shorter-lived mispricings that increase portfolio turnover by 34\%. This extra turnover leads to deeper net losses, highlighting how algorithmic sophistication cannot compensate for structural trading friction \citep{novymarx2016taxonomy}.
    \item \textbf{Utility as Execution Timing Overlays:} Although standalone daily statistical arbitrage is unviable in Indian single-stock F\&O, the residual signals retain clear practical value for execution management. Institutions and long-only funds can utilize Ornstein-Uhlenbeck residual displacement to pace planned orders, potentially capturing 20 to 40 basis points in execution improvement.
\end{enumerate}

From a market design perspective, the October 2024 increase in Securities Transaction Tax raises the cost threshold for quantitative market-neutral strategies. As statutory trading costs increase, high-turnover arbitrage capital scales back, with potential consequences for relative-value price discovery across Indian equity derivatives.
